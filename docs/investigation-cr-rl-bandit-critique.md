Grounded against the deployed skill at `~/.claude/skills/cross-review`.

**Correction (caught by cross-review):** an earlier draft of this doc claimed the in-repo `cross-review/` copy was "stale — missing `leaderboard.sh`, `select_roster.sh`, `anchor_findings.sh`, `factcheck_findings.sh`, `import_runlog.sh`, `tests/`." That was false — all six exist in-repo (verified: `git ls-tree -r HEAD -- cross-review/`). The deployed and in-repo copies do differ in content (`leaderboard.sh`: 185 vs. 186 lines) but the specific lines this critique cites (e.g. `leaderboard.sh:19-22`) are byte-identical between the two, so every citation below is checkable directly against the in-repo copy — no need to reach for the machine-local deployed path.

Files read in full: `scripts/leaderboard.sh` (185 lines), `scripts/select_roster.sh` (283 lines), `scripts/analyze_runlog.sh` (249 lines), `references/reviewer_profiles.json` (283 lines), plus `runlog.jsonl` (649 lines, 645 structured entries).

## Verdict

The proposal's core diagnosis is correct and not speculative — it's already written down as a `KNOWN LIMITATION` comment in `leaderboard.sh` itself. But roughly a third of the proposed machinery already exists in a simpler form under different names, and the heavier machinery (hierarchical Bayesian posteriors, true Thompson Sampling, Shapley-style complementarity, full RL controller) is scoped for a system with far more data and a different implementation substrate than this one has. This is a ~1000-line bash+jq telemetry system processing on the order of tens of runs per window (default `--recent 40`), not a production ML platform. Fitting a hierarchical Bayesian model per (reviewer × language × repo × risk-area × diff-size-bucket) cell over 645 total structured entries spread across 15 reviewers would leave most cells with 0-2 samples — the shrinkage-to-prior math the proposal describes is the right shape, but there's nothing to shrink from yet, and bash/jq is the wrong tool to fit it in regardless.

## Confirmed correct: the convergence-as-reward feedback loop

The proposal's central claim — agreement gets baked into score, score drives selection, selection creates more chances to agree, so a unique-but-correct reviewer looks bad until corroborated — is exactly what `leaderboard.sh:19-22` already documents:

> KNOWN LIMITATION: signal rewards agreeing with the crowd — a reviewer that uniquely finds real bugs scores low on signal until others corroborate. That's the price of having no ground truth; don't tune a reviewer out of the fleet on signal alone, look at its actual findings first.

The scoring formula (`leaderboard.sh:138-140`) is `score = 100*(0.45*reliability + 0.35*(convergent/findings) + 0.20*(1-dropped/findings))`. 35% of score is literally "did other reviewers agree with you." The proposal is right that this is a popularity contest dressed as a precision proxy, and the codebase's own comments already flag it as unsolved, not merely undiscovered.

## Already built, different vocabulary

The proposal presents several mechanisms as novel that are already implemented, just without the bandit terminology:

- **Exploration bonus / rookie prior** — `select_roster.sh:12-17`: `weight = max(score,15) * (1 + 0.5/sqrt(attempts+1)) / (1+p50/240) / (1+cost/pivot) * draw_boost`. The `0.5/sqrt(attempts+1)` term is a UCB-style exploration bonus that decays with sample count; `leaderboard.sh` gives never-run reviewers a rookie score of 50 rather than 0. This is the proposal's "reasonable prior, high uncertainty, meaningful exploration probability" — just a point-estimate heuristic rather than a sampled posterior.
- **Weighted random draw over a constrained roster** — `select_roster.sh`'s `draw_picks()` does weighted sampling without replacement, with hard constraints already matching the proposal's §5/§6 wishlist almost verbatim: codex+kimi always on (`baseline: true`), floor of 3 total reviewers, cost and latency shape the *draw* but never the *synthesis* weighting (explicitly called out as "Gabriel's speed-without-quality-loss constraint" in the comments) — this is the proposal's "hard constraints the policy must satisfy," already hand-coded.
- **Provider independence / redundancy avoidance** — `reviewer_profiles.json`'s `_synthesis_rules.provider_independence` already encodes the exact complementarity concern from §5: antigravity and gemini-pro are both Google/Gemini and their agreement is explicitly downweighted to "one provider vote," and OpenRouter is explicitly documented as a router, not a provider, so glm/deepseek/nemotron etc. are each counted as their true upstream lab. This is hand-authored pairwise redundancy knowledge — not a learned `C_ij` term, but the same problem, already solved for the one correlated pair that currently exists in the fleet.
- **Severity-aware trust rules** — `severity_priors` per reviewer (`high_precision`, `trust_if_convergent`, `verify`, `skip_unless_convergent`) already differentiate how much a solo finding should be trusted by severity, which is adjacent to (though not the same as) the proposal's severity-weighted reward term.
- **Quota/degradation handling** — the 0.1x quota penalty and sleep-suspect timeout filtering in `select_roster.sh`/`analyze_runlog.sh` are a more mature nonstationarity/reliability treatment than the proposal discusses at all.

## Real, unaddressed gaps the proposal correctly identifies

- **No canonical per-finding lifecycle.** `runlog.jsonl` tracks `findings_total`/`findings_convergent`/`findings_dropped` as *per-reviewer counts*, not per-finding records with reviewer attribution, anchor status, and downstream disposition. There's no `finding_id`, no tracking of human-accepted/fix-applied/fix-verified/regression state. This is the proposal's strongest, most actionable point — the current data model cannot answer "was this specific finding eventually right," only "how many of this reviewer's findings converged this round."
- **No severity weighting in the score itself.** `severity_priors` govern trust-at-synthesis-time, but `leaderboard.sh`'s score treats every finding as equal regardless of Critical/High/Medium/Low. A reviewer that finds one real Critical and three noise Lows scores identically (on the findings axis) to one that finds four Lows.
- **No unique-discovery credit.** This is the direct fix for the feedback loop, and it's completely absent. A solo-but-later-verified finding currently has no path to boosting `convergent` — the schema doesn't even have a "verified independently" bucket. The proposal's discovery-pool/corroboration-pool split is a real, well-targeted fix, not present in any form.
- **No baseline-incremental measurement.** Nothing currently computes "what did this rotation reviewer find that codex+kimi didn't." Codex and kimi being fixed baselines makes this an easy diff to compute (their findings are known for every round) but it isn't computed anywhere today.
- **Selection decisions aren't durably logged.** `select_roster.sh` prints per-candidate weights and the seed to *stderr* for the human/log tail, but `append_runlog.sh`'s schema (checked directly) has no `--seed`/`--weights`/`--roster` fields — the propensities needed for off-policy evaluation are computed and then thrown away every round. This is a genuine, cheap-to-fix gap: the data already exists in the process, it's just not being captured to `runlog.jsonl`.
- **No delayed-feedback / fix-verification loop.** Fact-check (`factcheck_findings.sh`) is the only post-hoc verification stage, and it isn't wired back into reviewer scoring at all (leaderboard's `dropped` count is fact-check disproof, which *is* used — but there's nothing beyond that: no human-acceptance signal, no fix-applied/fix-verified/regression tracking).

## Where the proposal is overscoped for this system

- **Hierarchical Bayesian posteriors per context cell** — the proposal's context vector (language, repo, diff-size bucket, docs fraction, risk area, agentic-vs-diff-only, pass number...) crossed with 15 reviewers over 645 total entries produces mostly-empty cells. There isn't a "provider-family precision" layer with enough members to shrink toward (most non-Google providers are a fleet of one). This part of the proposal assumes a scale this system doesn't have and isn't likely to reach soon on personal-project cross-review volume.
- **True Thompson Sampling (posterior sampling)** vs. what exists (point-estimate score + explicit sqrt-decay exploration term, then weighted random draw) — the practical behavior is similar (explore-heavy early, exploit-heavy once `attempts` is large), and implementing actual posterior sampling in bash/jq is impractical; it would require moving the scoring layer to Python (or at minimum a language with a stats library), which is a bigger architectural change than the proposal frames it as.
- **Shapley/complementarity (`C_ij`) modeling** — reasonable in principle, but with only one currently-known correlated pair (antigravity/gemini-pro, already hand-handled) there's no evidence yet that pairwise learned complementarity would outperform the existing hardcoded provider-independence rule.
- **Full RL controller for auto-fix/multi-pass** — directionally correct that this is a genuinely different (sequential, state-changing) problem, and correctly recommended for *later*, offline-first, shadow-mode before deployment. No objection here; just noting it's speculative future work relative to points above.

## Recommended scope if you want to act on this

If the goal is to fix the feedback loop with the least architectural risk, the highest-value/lowest-cost slice of the proposal, mapped onto real files:

1. **Log roster decisions durably** — thread `select_roster.sh`'s already-computed seed/weights/candidates into `append_runlog.sh` as a new optional field block. Nearly free; the data is computed, just discarded today.
2. **Add a `finding_id` + per-finding record** to the fact-check/synthesis pipeline (`anchor_findings.sh` → `factcheck_findings.sh` → SKILL.md synthesis) instead of only aggregate counts. This is the prerequisite for everything else (severity weighting, unique-discovery credit, baseline-incremental value) and is a schema change, not a modeling change.
3. **Add severity weight + unique-vs-corroborated split to `leaderboard.sh`'s score formula** once (1) and (2) exist — this directly defuses the feedback loop the proposal (and the code's own comment) flags, without needing a Bayesian layer at all: a simple `score = f(severity-weighted unique finds, severity-weighted corroborated finds, reliability)` recomputation is a same-language (jq) change.
4. Leave the Bayesian/contextual-bandit/RL layers (proposal §1, §6, §11, and the full RL controller) as explicitly deferred — revisit only if/when per-reviewer sample counts grow enough that point estimates are visibly noisy in practice (leaderboard already surfaces `attempts` per reviewer; watch that number).
